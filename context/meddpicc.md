# MEDDPICC: what each field means and what counts as evidence

DRAFT by Claude approved by Taha. Taha owns this file. The agent reads it on every call,
and its hash goes into the audit log, so an edit here is visible in every later proposal.

## How to write a value

- Write what the call established, in one or two plain sentences. No opinion, no advice.
- Name people with their title the first time: "Priya Raman, Director of IT Infrastructure".
- Keep numbers exactly as spoken. Do not round, convert or add units.
- If the call does not establish a field, leave the field out. An empty field is correct;
  a guessed one is not. The rep will ask on the next call.

## Metrics

**Definition.** The measurable business result the buyer expects from the purchase.

**Counts as evidence.** A number the buyer states about today's cost or a target: hours
lost, tickets per month, days to onboard, a percentage they want to reach.

**Does not count.** A number the seller offers. Adjectives without a number ("much
faster", "a lot of downtime"). The price of the deal.

**Example.** "Three VPN outages last quarter, about four hours each. Wants VPN tickets
down from about 400 a month by 70 percent."

## Economic Buyer

**Definition.** The one person who can approve the spend and can say yes when others say no.

**Counts as evidence.** A named person, with a statement from them or from the buyer's
side that they sign or control the budget for this purchase.

**Does not count.** "Budget sits above me" with no name. A senior title with no stated
authority over this spend. The champion, unless they say they sign.

**Example.** "Elena Vasquez, CFO. Signs any contract above 50,000 dollars."

## Decision Criteria

**Definition.** The requirements the buyer will judge every option against.

**Counts as evidence.** Requirements the buyer states as must-haves or as how they will
compare vendors: technical, commercial or compliance.

**Does not count.** Features the seller lists. Things the buyer merely finds interesting.

**Example.** "Must work with the existing single sign-on, give contractors access per
application, need no hardware in the depots, and come with a SOC 2 report."

## Decision Process

**Definition.** The steps and people between now and a decision, in order, with dates
where given.

**Counts as evidence.** Named steps the buyer commits to: a pilot with a scope and an end
date, a review meeting, a committee, who decides after whom.

**Does not count.** A vague intention ("we would probably want to try it first"). The
legal and procurement steps, which belong in Paper Process.

**Example.** "Two-week pilot in the Reno depot, then Priya Raman recommends to the CFO,
who decides at the January 14 board meeting."

## Paper Process

**Definition.** How a decision becomes a signed contract: legal, security, procurement,
and who signs.

**Counts as evidence.** The buyer naming the documents, reviews, systems and people
involved, and how long each takes.

**Does not count.** The seller's own contracting steps. A wish for a signature date with
no steps behind it.

**Example.** "Master agreement goes to the General Counsel, two to three weeks for
redlines. Security questionnaire first. Vendor must be set up in the procurement portal
and a purchase order raised before signature."

## Identified Pain

**Definition.** The problem that makes the buyer act now, and what it costs them.

**Counts as evidence.** The buyer describing a problem in their own words, with a
consequence or a deadline.

**Does not count.** A problem the seller suggests and the buyer does not confirm.
General dissatisfaction with no consequence.

**Example.** "VPN hardware goes out of support in March. During outages dispatchers are
locked out and trucks sit idle."

## Champion

**Definition.** A person inside the buyer with influence who is actively selling for us
when we are not in the room.

**Counts as evidence.** A named person who has taken or commits to a concrete action on
our behalf: raising it with the budget owner, running the evaluation, arguing for it
internally.

**Does not count.** Someone who is friendly or interested. Someone who attends calls but
commits to nothing.

**Example.** "Priya Raman, Director of IT Infrastructure. Running the evaluation and has
committed to take the proposal to the CFO."

## Competition

**Definition.** Every alternative the buyer is weighing, including doing nothing and
building it themselves.

**Counts as evidence.** The buyer naming another vendor, the incumbent, an internal
build, or the option of staying as they are.

**Does not count.** The seller's guess at who else is involved. An unnamed "other
vendor" with nothing else said about it.

**Example.** "Renewing the existing VPN contract is the default option. One other vendor
is being evaluated; the buyer declined to name it."
